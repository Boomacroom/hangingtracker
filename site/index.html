How jurisdictions classify hanging deaths

A hanging death can be ruled suicide, homicide, or undetermined intent. Which one gets recorded varies several-fold between states. Here is that variation, what this data can and cannot say about why, and the reason almost all of it is invisible unless you query it a particular way.

Rate of undetermined-intent classification

Does it matter who certifies the death?

The readiest explanation for that variation is structural. Some states elect county coroners who need no medical training; some run a centralized medical examiner office staffed by forensic pathologists; most are a mixture. We joined CDC's county-level table of who certifies deaths, weighted by how many deaths each county actually certifies, and tested it against the rate above.

This data cannot answer it. Across 20 measurable states, the share of deaths certified by an elected official has no relationship with how often intent is left undetermined (). Coroner share shows a weak positive trend that is not significant, and dropping any single state moves it anywhere from p = 0.04 to p = 0.20. A result decided by one observation is not a result. An earlier version of this page reported a clean null here; correcting the age contamination removed nine states from the measurable set and took the statistical power with them.
Undetermined rate against certification structure

Twenty states is too few to settle this either way, most states are unmeasurable, and a state labelled "mixed" is an average of counties that differ from each other. The variation is real; what explains it is open, and may live at the level of individual offices rather than state law — caseload, autopsy rate, local convention, or how one large county certifies.

Undetermined-intent rate by group
Why 15+, and why this was not published sooner. On all-ages data the same query showed a much larger gap. These ICD-10 codes cover suffocation as well as hanging, and —% of all undetermined-intent deaths are children under 5 — whose suicide count is structurally zero, so they add to the numerator and nothing to the denominator. Infant suffocation mortality differs sharply between groups, so roughly half the apparent gap was that and not classification practice. The age-filtered figures above are what survives the correction, and they are what gets published.

Case records are compiled from published reporting and are frozen. This dataset records what authorities ruled and who has disputed it. It does not assess whether any ruling is correct.
